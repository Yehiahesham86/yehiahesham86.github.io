Training · Welcome

Welcome to the TMK Service Evaluation Project

This handbook is designed for existing TMK team members. It explains not only what to do during every service evaluation call, but why this project exists, why TMK was selected, and how your work contributes to customer experience and referral growth.

Why was this project created?

The former CM model proved that service evaluation conversations naturally generate high-quality referrals. This project rebuilds that model by focusing on service evaluation, issue resolution, and referral activation only after customer satisfaction is confirmed.

Why TMK?

  • The team already understands 51Talk products and customers.
  • The project can launch quickly without creating a new team.
  • The project creates an opportunity to earn additional income through the incentive program tied to high-quality referrals and project performance.

Your Mission

  • Evaluate the customer's learning experience.
  • Identify satisfaction and service issues.
  • Resolve issues whenever possible.
  • Build trust before discussing referrals.
  • Collect valuable feedback for continuous improvement.

Project Philosophy

Service → Trust → Satisfaction → Referral → Attendance → Growth

A referral is not the objective of the call—it is the natural outcome of an excellent customer experience.

What We Expect From Every TMK Member

  • Put customer experience first.
  • Listen before recommending.
  • Resolve before escalating whenever possible.
  • Never force a referral.
  • Document every interaction accurately.
  • Represent the Service Evaluation Team professionally.

Why This Project Exists

Across the Middle East, there's a structural gap in referrals: the original CM team proved that service calls naturally trigger referrals, but the newer SS team's reach alone can't sustain that same level of output.

~40
referral leads per CM agent, historically
~4
orders generated per CM agent

This isn't a new experiment starting from zero — it's a proven model. The real work is fixing the surrounding pieces: onboarding, performance appraisal, service-access permissions, and standardized process.

So the core of this project isn't "add a team that asks for referrals." It's building a service-evaluation team dedicated to paying users — one that identifies satisfaction, resolves real issues, and lets high-quality referrals happen as a natural result.

Training · Project Goal

What This Project Is Trying to Achieve

Official positioning

The project is standardized as the Service Evaluation Project, and the team as the Service Evaluation Team — not a pure referral unit, not a conventional customer-service team. Its core mission: confirm user experience through standardized service calls, identify satisfied users, resolve issues for dissatisfied ones, and trigger high-quality referrals at the right moment.

Why "service evaluation," not "referral invitation"

In Saudi Arabia, service-evaluation calls are already a familiar format — STC and Zain both call customers this way after a purchase. Using "service evaluation" as the entry point, instead of leading with a referral ask, reduces resistance to sales outreach and lets the team prioritize solving problems over pushing for referrals.

Why TMK was chosen

TMK was chosen for Phase 1 because the team is already in place internally, already understands 51Talk's business closely, fits directly into the projects — while giving the team itself a real opportunity to earn more through the referral incentive structure.

Your Role — Five Responsibilities

Everything in the script library maps back to these five responsibilities. Understanding the "why" behind each one makes the scripts easier to use naturally.

1

Service Experience Evaluation

Run service-evaluation calls with paying users to assess their recent learning experience: course schedule, teacher satisfaction, service responsiveness, attendance, overall satisfaction.

2

Handling Dissatisfied Users

If a user is dissatisfied, don't just log it — you're empowered to actually resolve it. Prioritize fixing the issue before deciding whether a referral is appropriate.

If a call can only log a problem without fixing it, the experience won't improve — and word-of-mouth referrals won't happen.

3

Referral Trigger from Satisfied Users

For users with high satisfaction, stable learning experience, and no complaints, the trigger follows a natural chain: confirm experience → assess satisfaction → reinforce learning value → naturally ask about friends or relatives → capture the referral → follow up on attendance and conversion.

4

Check-in / Share Fallback

If a user is satisfied but can't name a referral right now, guide them to at least one check-in or share. It keeps them engaged, adds natural exposure, creates future referral opportunities, and removes the pressure of needing a referral on every single call.

5

User Experience Project Support

TMK can also take on staged research tasks for the User Experience team — phone interviews or service surveys for specific user segments. The add-on probes for this are already built into Section 8 of the script library.

The Full Standard Operating Procedure

This is the exact sequence every case follows, end to end. Each script in this book maps to one of these steps.

  1. User list is entered into the system.
  2. TMK checks the user's status and service context.
  3. Call the service-evaluation hotline. → §1
  4. Assess user satisfaction. → §2
  5. Satisfied users → trigger the referral flow. → §3
  6. Satisfied users unwilling to give a referral → automatically counted as a check-in or share. → §4
  7. Dissatisfied users → routed to the issue-resolution process. → §5
  8. Issues that can be resolved on the spot are handled by TMK within its authorized scope.
  9. Complex issues are escalated to CC, SS, Customer Service, or the relevant owner. → §5.7
  10. Daily accumulation of call data: satisfaction scores, issues, check-ins, referrals, and attendance. → §9
  11. Weekly review of issue types, referral quality, attendance-to-conversion rates, SOP execution, and incentive effectiveness.

How You'll Be Measured

Deliberately, "number of referrals" is not the core metric. Chasing raw referral counts leads to low-quality leads and unnecessary interruption of users — so performance shifts from "how many" to "do they actually show up."

Indicator typeIndicatorPurpose
Outcome (North Star)Referral attendance targetEnsures quality of referrals, not just registration count
OutcomeCheck-in participation rateEnsures users can contribute even without a referral
ProcessValid call volume, answer rate, evaluation completion rateEnsures basic activity and coverage
ServiceIssue resolution rate, escalation timeliness, satisfactionEnsures the team genuinely serves, not just refers
ConversionAttendance rate, attendance-payment rate, referral-payment rateAssesses business contribution of referrals

Two incentive tiers

Tier 1 — process incentives: built around effective call volume, evaluation completion, check-in participation, and issue-resolution completion.

Tier 2 — outcome incentives: built around referral attendance, post-attendance paid contribution, and valid order contribution.

The goal: make it worthwhile to both make the calls and register high-quality referrals — without chasing referral counts alone.

The Bigger Picture

The value of this project is building a new handover mechanism — service evaluation, issue resolution, high-quality referral triggering, and user-experience accumulation — in a gap the existing team can't fully cover alone.

This isn't about asking TMK to chase referral leads. It's about positioning TMK as a genuine service-evaluation team: assess experience first, fix real problems for dissatisfied users, let satisfied users trigger referrals naturally, and guide everyone else toward a check-in or share.

Service evaluation → Issue resolution → Satisfaction identification → High-quality referrals → Check-in/share fallback → Attendance/payment conversion → User experience accumulation → Continuous iteration.
01

Opening & Service Evaluation

Confirm identity, set a low-pressure tone, and move into the satisfaction check. Referral is never mentioned here — in Saudi, an unprompted evaluation call is a familiar, trusted format (STC and Zain both call customers this way), so lead with that familiarity, not with a pitch.

Opening line

Hello, this is Rania, a Service Evaluation Specialist at 51Talk. Am I speaking with Umm Faisal, parent of Faisal? I'm calling today simply to check in on your child's recent learning experience — it will only take a couple of minutes.

If the parent seems rushed

I understand you're busy — this isn't a sales call, it's a short service check we do for active families. I just have three quick questions and then I'll let you go.

Sample Call — routine openingRecorded 04:12
R
Rania · AgentHello, this is Rania from 51Talk's Service Evaluation team — am I speaking with Umm Faisal?
U
Umm Faisal · ParentYes, speaking. Is everything okay?
R
Rania · AgentEverything's fine — I'm just calling to check how Faisal's classes have been going lately, similar to the service calls you might get from STC. Do you have two minutes?
U
Umm Faisal · ParentAh, okay — yes, go ahead.

Coaching note: naming a familiar local reference point (STC/Zain-style service calls) is what gets the tone accepted in the first 10 seconds.

02

Satisfaction Assessment Probes

Five dimensions build the standard call output: satisfaction level, main feedback, whether there's an issue, whether referral is appropriate, and whether follow-up is needed. The last question is the branch point.

DimensionProbeListening for
AttendanceHow consistent has attendance been recently?Frequent absences, scheduling conflicts
TeacherHow does your child feel about the current teacher?Rapport, requests to switch
ScheduleDoes the current schedule and level still work well?Overload, mismatch
ResponsivenessWhen you've reached out before, how was the response?Unresolved past tickets
OverallOverall, how would you describe the experience — very satisfied, satisfied, or not satisfied?Direct branch signal

Branch point: a clearly positive answer → Section 3. Any hesitation or complaint → Section 5.

03
Branch A · Satisfied

Referral Trigger Script

Enter only once satisfaction is clearly confirmed and there is no open issue. Sequence: experience confirmation → satisfaction → learning-value reinforcement → natural referral ask → referral capture → reward confirmation.

Learning-value reinforcement + ask

I'm really glad to hear Faisal has been having a good experience. Feedback like yours, based on real day-to-day use, genuinely helps other parents decide whether to try us. Do you have any friends or family members who've also been thinking about English lessons for their child?

If a name is given

That's great — could I take their name and number? Our team will reach out personally, mention that you recommended them, and confirm your reward once they register, per the current referral terms.

Sample Call — referral capturedOutcome: Referral logged
R
Rania · AgentIt sounds like things have been going really well for Faisal — I'm glad to hear that. Feedback like yours actually helps other parents a lot. Is there anyone in your family or among friends who's been considering English lessons?
U
Umm Faisal · ParentActually yes — my sister was just asking me about this last week.
R
Rania · AgentWonderful — could I take her name and number? Our team will reach out, mention it's from you, and I'll make sure your referral reward is confirmed once she registers.
U
Umm Faisal · ParentSure, it's Aisha, 05X-XXX-XXXX.
R
Rania · AgentThank you so much — I've noted Aisha as your referral, and our team will follow up within three days.

Never quote a specific reward figure without confirming the referral terms currently in effect.

Check-in / Share Fallback

Use when the parent is satisfied but has no referral on hand. The goal is to secure at least a check-in or share — a call is never a dead end.

That's completely fine — if no one specific comes to mind right now, would you be willing to help us with a quick check-in or share about Faisal's experience instead? It takes less than a minute and helps other parents see genuine feedback.

Branch B · Dissatisfied

Issue Resolution Scripts

The moment dissatisfaction surfaces, stop the evaluation flow and move to resolution. No referral talk in this branch — resolve first, revisit referral potential on a future call.

General acknowledgment

Thank you for letting me know — I want to make sure we get this right for you. Let me look into this and see what I can do directly, or make sure it reaches the right person.

Issue typeScript
Occasional leaveI can process an occasional leave for you now — could you confirm the date(s) and the reason?
Tutoring / extra helpI can arrange a tutoring session to help with that — let me check what's available and get this scheduled.
Schedule adjustmentI can help adjust the schedule — what times would work better going forward?
Teacher changeI can submit a request to review the teacher assignment and flag your feedback for a better fit.
CompensationGiven what you've described, you qualify for one additional lesson as a gesture to make this right.

S
Umm Sara · ParentHonestly, the class time hasn't worked for us in weeks — Sara's been missing half of them.
R

S
Umm Sara · ParentEvenings after 7pm would be much better.
R

5.7 · Outside permission scope

Escalation Script

This is something I want to make sure gets the right attention, so I'm escalating it directly to our CC / SS / Customer Service team today. You'll hear back within a few business days, and I'll personally note this so it isn't lost.

Rule: never promise an outcome outside the permissions in Section VI of the project doc (outbound tool, CRM query, referral registration, leave/make-up, class/teacher assistance, escalation mechanism). Escalate rather than guess.

09

Call Data Logging

  • Call outcome — connected / no answer / call back requested
  • Satisfaction level captured
  • Main feedback (1–2 lines, free text)
  • Service issue identified, and type
  • Issue resolved on-call, escalated, or unresolved
  • Referral triggered — name/contact if provided
  • Check-in / share completed as fallback
  • Follow-up required, and by whom
10

Quick Reference Card

1. Open with service evaluation — never lead with referral.

2. Assess satisfaction across the 5 probe dimensions.

3. Satisfied → referral ask, or check-in/share if no referral.

4. Dissatisfied → resolve within permissions, or escalate.

5. Close with the matching script, then log the call.

Golden rule: resolve before you ask — and never end a satisfied call empty-handed.
